How voice measurement works

A guided tour of the ideas inside voice-core. Each step has one idea and one thing to try. Tags show what the library does today: in voice-core or planned.

1. Sound as numbers in voice-core

A recording is a list of numbers (samples) between -1 and 1, plus a sample rate: how many samples per second. voice-core takes exactly this: mono f32 samples and a rate.

Highest pitch the recording can represent: 8000 Hz. Human speech energy sits mostly below 4 kHz, which is why 16 kHz is plenty.

Predict: what rate would you need to capture a 10 kHz sound? Then drag the slider to check.

2. Frames and hops in voice-core

Speech changes over time, so measurements are made on short slices called frames (25 ms) that slide forward by a hop (10 ms). In the library: dsp::frames, and dsp::resample to normalise to 16 kHz.

With a 25 ms frame and 10 ms hop you get 198 frames, so 198 numbers per measurement.

Predict: if the hop became 20 ms, how many frames would a 2 s clip have?

3. Loudness in voice-core

rms_db and frame_rms_db give level in dBFS (0 is digital full scale, quieter is negative). integrated_lufs follows the broadcast standard and ignores silence, so pauses do not drag the value down. Phone mics are uncalibrated, so these are relative: compare yourself to yourself on the same device.

Level: -20.0 dBFS. Doubling amplitude adds about 6 dB.

Predict: what amplitude gives -40 dBFS?

4. Pitch in voice-core

Pitch (f0) is how many times per second your vocal folds vibrate, in Hz. Only voiced sounds (vowels, "m", "z") have pitch; "s" and silence do not. Because we hear pitch on a ratio scale, we report semitones relative to 100 Hz. voice-core tracks pitch frame by frame and then picks the most plausible path across frames, which avoids most "octave jumps" (reporting double the true pitch). It has been compared with Praat on synthetic signals and on eight real speech clips.

= 12.0 semitones above 100 Hz.

Predict: going from 100 Hz to 200 Hz is 12 semitones. How many semitones from 200 to 400 Hz?

5. Pauses in voice-core

Frames within 25 dB of your loudest frame count as speech; the rest is silence. A silent gap of at least 0.1 s between two stretches of speech is a pause. Silence at the very start and end is not a pause. voice-core reports the count, total pause time and the longest pause, and agrees with Praat's silence detection on eight real clips. Because the threshold is relative to your loudest frame, one loud click in a quiet recording can hide the speech.

6. Pace in voice-core (early)

Words per minute needs a transcript, so the library measures syllables per second by detecting syllable peaks (de Jong and Wempe, 2009). The number voice-core reports today divides by the whole recording, so pauses pull it down; a version that excludes pauses (articulation rate) is not in the library yet. So far it has only been checked on synthetic speech-like bursts, not real speech.

7. Steadiness: jitter, shimmer, HNR planned

Jitter: cycle-to-cycle wobble in pitch. Shimmer: cycle-to-cycle wobble in loudness. HNR: harmonics-to-noise ratio in dB, higher means a clearer, less breathy tone. They are noisy measures, best on a sustained vowel ("aaah"). Not a medical test.

8. Reports and warnings in voice-core

A measurement can be wrong without looking wrong. One call returns the whole report as a serialisable struct, together with warnings so an app can say why a number is unreliable instead of showing it confidently. Today the warnings are: recording too short (under 1 s), clipped, and too quiet. A "too noisy" warning is planned. The thresholds are practical defaults, not from a standard.

9. Streaming vs batch in voice-core (early)

Batch: analyse the whole take after you stop. Streaming: feed small chunks of any size and get a level and a speech/silence flag for each 10 ms frame almost immediately, for a live meter. The streaming speech flag is rougher than the batch pauses (it agrees with Praat on about 88% of frames), so use batch for final numbers. Live pitch and pace in streaming are planned.

10. For developers

  • No network, no model files, builds for desktop, Android, iOS and wasm32-unknown-unknown.
  • MIT/Apache/BSD dependencies only, enforced by cargo deny.
  • Praat is GPL-3, so it is used only as an offline test oracle (via Parselmouth); algorithms are reimplemented from papers and listed in PROVENANCE.md.
