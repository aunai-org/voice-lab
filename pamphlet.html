Your voice, in numbers

A one-page guide. voice-core measures these parts of a recording, on your device, with no internet.

What you hearThe numberWhat it tells you
How high or lowPitch (Hz, semitones)Your usual range and how much you vary. Flat pitch can sound monotone.
How loudLoudness (dBFS, LUFS)Compare your own takes on the same phone. Relative, not true volume.
How fastPace (syllables/s)Steady beats fast.
Where you stopPausesShort, deliberate pauses sound confident; long ones show hesitation.
How steadyJitter, shimmer, HNRTiny wobbles in pitch and loudness, and how clear vs breathy the tone is.

Three ideas to remember

  1. Frames: audio is cut into 25 ms slices, measured per slice, then summarised.
  2. Relative, not absolute: phone mics are uncalibrated, so compare yourself to yourself.
  3. Warnings matter: if a take is too short, noisy or clipped, the library says so instead of giving a confident wrong number.
What this is not: a medical test, speech recognition, or a judge of your accent or emotion.
Try it: read aloud for 30 seconds, twice. Change one thing (slow down, or speak closer to the mic) and compare.
